# The games worth the work

Proteus gives a game module only to games that people still name when they are asked for
the best of the Atari 2600. This is that list, how it was made, and what each game will ask
of a module.

## How the list was made

Nineteen lists of the best or the defining games of the console were read in full in
September 2026: ranked lists, unranked ones, and every game a long-running review site
grades A- or better. A game's place is the number of lists that name it; games named
equally often are in the order of how high the ranked lists place them. Sales are from
Wikipedia's list of best-selling Atari 2600 games and count for nothing in the order.

What this cannot tell:

- AtariAge's polls could not be read (the site turns automated readers away), nor Retro
  Gamer's top 25 of 2008, which is not online. From what is quoted of it, Pitfall II,
  H.E.R.O. and Solaris would each stand higher.
- Two of the lists are of games that *defined* the console, which is why Pac-Man is on them.
  E.T. is on three lists for the same reason and on none for its merits, and is left out.
- From place 37 on, every game is named by two lists: their order means little.

The first six stand apart from the rest, on 13 lists and more. Places 7 to 11 are a second

Controllers are RetroArch's business and not Proteus's, so paddles stand in no module's way.
They are noted because a game that needs them is tried with them.

## Why Pac-Man came second, from place 13

It is the console's best-selling game and its most scolded: reviews of its day and since
name the ghosts that flicker and look alike, the sounds, and the colours. Every one of
those is a matter of picture and sound, and none of how the game plays. No game on the
list has more to gain from what Proteus does.

## What the games have in common

A module is made of what `docs/GAME_MODULES.md` calls patterns. Grouped by what they will
need, the first twenty fall into few kinds, and most of what a kind needs is made once.

| Kind | Games | What it needs | Made? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rows of enemies over a cannon | Space Invaders, Demon Attack, Megamania, Galaxian, Phoenix | rows told apart, a backdrop, shots and hits | yes: Space Invaders |
| A maze and what roams it | Pac-Man, Ms. Pac-Man, Berzerk, Wizard of Wor, Dig Dug | things shown in turns told apart, walls recoloured and lit, what is eaten drawn anew | yes: Pac-Man |
| Rooms, one screen at a time | Adventure, Pitfall!, H.E.R.O., Haunted House, Montezuma's Revenge, Raiders of the Lost Ark | the room known from memory, a look for every kind of room, light and dark | no |
| Land that scrolls | River Raid, Stargate, Defender, The Empire Strikes Back, Jungle Hunt, Moon Patrol | a backdrop that moves with the land, in layers | no |
| A road into the distance | Pole Position, Enduro | the horizon and the time of day from the game's colours | no |
| Rocks and ships in space | Asteroids, Yars' Revenge, Solaris, Gravitar, Cosmic Ark | many things shown in turns, trails, a backdrop | mostly: Space Invaders |
| A field and a bat | Warlords, Kaboom!, Breakout, Video Olympics | what is hit bursts; little else | mostly |
| Two players against each other | Combat, Joust | sounds placed by who makes them | mostly |

Two things no module has needed yet will be needed soon:

- **A backdrop that moves with the game.** River Raid and Pitfall! tell in memory how far
  the land has moved.
- **Sound that is not the TIA's.** Pitfall II has a chip of its own for its music. Proteus
  hears the mix and cannot take its voices apart.
